The histogram becomes useful to a kernel engineer when every count has a matrix
interpretation. If expert E receives M tokens, a gate or up projection multiplies
an `[M, H]` activation matrix by an `[H, F]` weight matrix. The down projection
multiplies `[M, F]` by `[F, H]`.

Our compiler represents the gate, up, and down projections separately. It does
not count activation functions, routing, memory transfers, or scatter operations
as GEMM arithmetic.

## A small calculation we can check

Take our routed histogram `[2, 2, 3, 1]` and two shared experts receiving four
tokens each. Set H to 3 and F to 5. Across all six experts, M sums to 16.
Three matrix multiplications require:

```text
useful GEMM FLOPs = 3 × 2 × 16 × 3 × 5 = 1440
```

With a tile of M=2, N=4, K=2, padded M sums to 18. Gate and up pad N to 8 and K
to 4; down pads N to 4 and K to 6. The padded arithmetic model becomes:

```text
gate + up = 2 × (2 × 18 × 8 × 4) = 2304
down      =     2 × 18 × 4 × 6  =  864
total                            = 3168
```

That difference is analytical padding, not a measured instruction count. Masked
kernels avoid invalid reads and writes, but a partial tile can still consume
matrix arithmetic capacity.

## Tiles and waves need assumptions

An output tile count is `ceil(M / BM) × ceil(N / BN)`. K controls the reduction
iterations rather than multiplying the number of output CTAs. Confusing those
two dimensions would inflate our scheduling estimate.

For this example, the projections contain 18, 18, and 9 output tiles.
With capacity for two CTAs at once, our simple model gives 9, 9, and 5 waves.
Each projection is a separate launch; we cannot combine their remainders into
one shared wave calculation.

The persistent Triton implementation uses a fixed worker pool to visit those
tiles. Hardware occupancy also depends on registers, shared memory, scheduling,
and the actual compiled kernel. Our wave estimate does not model those effects.

## What changed

The compiler now emits explicit expert groups and dispatch rows, dimensions,
tile counts, padded FLOPs, configuration, assumptions, and a hash of its source
trace. Inactive experts have zero tiles and empty dispatch lists.

We also implement the calculation in the browser and compare its output against
Python-generated fixtures. Move the tile controls in the [lab](/lab/) to see why
the same routing event can imply different amounts of padded work.
